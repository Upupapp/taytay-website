OUR STORY
History & Heritage

The town's name is often traced to taytáy, an old Tagalog word for a bamboo or wooden footbridge. When floods forced the town uphill, it was renamed San Juan del Monte — but its people kept calling it Taytay.

Origins
Part of the Kingdom of Namayan
A settlement on the eastern shore of Laguna de Bay.
1579
Town and church founded
Taytay is formally established, with St. John the Baptist as patron.
1591
Up to San Juan del Monte
The Jesuits take over the parish. Under Fr. Pedro Chirino, the church moves from the flood-prone lakeshore up to a hill.
1599–1601
The first stone church
Taytay's church is built in stone for the first time.
1901
Province of Rizal
Act No. 137 creates the province on June 11, with Taytay among its towns.
1903
Merged, then restored
Act No. 942 merges Taytay with Cainta and Angono; both later regain their own towns.
1945
Liberation
Local soldiers and guerrillas free the town after three years of Japanese occupation.
1975
Stays with Rizal
When P.D. 824 forms Metro Manila, Taytay remains a town of Rizal.
2024
A basilica and a top-2 ranking
Taytay Church is declared the Minor Basilica of St. John the Baptist on July 9, and Taytay ranks 2nd among 1st- and 2nd-class municipalities in the national competitiveness index.
Today
2nd most populous municipality
397,111 residents in 2024 — and still the Garments and Woodworks Capital of the Philippines.
