Our Story

History & Heritage

The town's name is often traced to taytáy, an old Tagalog word for a bamboo or wooden footbridge. When floods forced the town uphill, it was renamed San Juan del Monte — but its people kept calling it Taytay.

  1. OriginsPart of the Kingdom of NamayanA settlement on the eastern shore of Laguna de Bay.
  2. 1579Town and church foundedTaytay is formally established, with St. John the Baptist as patron.
  3. 1591Up to San Juan del MonteThe Jesuits take over the parish. Under Fr. Pedro Chirino, the church moves from the flood-prone lakeshore up to a hill.
  4. 1599–1601The first stone churchTaytay's church is built in stone for the first time.
  5. 1901Province of RizalAct No. 137 creates the province on June 11, with Taytay among its towns.
  6. 1903Merged, then restoredAct No. 942 merges Taytay with Cainta and Angono; both later regain their own towns.
  7. 1945LiberationLocal soldiers and guerrillas free the town after three years of Japanese occupation.
  8. 1975Stays with RizalWhen P.D. 824 forms Metro Manila, Taytay remains a town of Rizal.
  9. 2024A basilica and a top-2 rankingTaytay Church is declared the Minor Basilica of St. John the Baptist on July 9, and Taytay ranks 2nd among 1st- and 2nd-class municipalities in the national competitiveness index.
  10. Today2nd most populous municipality397,111 residents in 2024 — and still the Garments and Woodworks Capital of the Philippines.
Symbols of the Town

The Municipal Seal

Its colors come from the Philippine flag. Together its symbols stand for HaMaKa — Hamba, Makina at Kabuhayan, which the town celebrates every year.
